### 8. Change course
The fleet changes course when evidence shows the plan is wrong, or when the product owner says the plan changed.
Every change of course goes through `compose change`, so it is recorded, and can be undone or updated.

**What wakes you.**
- `event reflect=<file> broken=N unclear=N trigger=...`: Jev screened every live line of Decisions so far against new evidence (a task that ended without passing, a contradiction, a verify FAIL) and flagged at least one.
- `task=NNN state=contradiction`: the implementer found a fact in its brief wrong and stopped.
- A teammate's `contradiction` report: something it found contradicts Linear.
- `event reflect=<file> error=...`: the screen could not run; judge the evidence yourself.
- The product owner's change of plans, from the engineering manager: run `compose reflect --project <slug> --evidence -` with their words to find what they touch.
- A layer you reject for a reason that may be the plan's: run `reflect --evidence -` with your reason.

**Then, in order.**
1. Hold first: `compose hold --project <slug> --only <ids> --reason "<one line>"` for every task whose brief rests on a flagged line. Tasks run in under a minute, so hold before you confirm; a hold is cheap to undo.
2. Steer any teammate whose work rests on the flagged line: `herdr agent prompt <slug>-<role> "stop <that part>; keep going on the rest"`.
3. Confirm: read the reflect file, the evidence at its file:line, and the Linear line. Jev is a screen, never the verdict. Then:

| It is | Do |
|---|---|
| a false alarm | `compose reflect --project <slug> --dismiss "<why>"`, `hold --release <ids>`, a one-line comment on the task, and `run --only NNN` for a `contradiction` task |
| task-sized | `compose reflect --project <slug> --dismiss "<why>"`, then step 7 |
| objective, inside the settled decisions (a missing `after:`, a split, a wrong owner) | re-plan yourself, below, with no page |
| complex, subjective, or a reversal of anything the product owner settled | escalate, below; everything stays held |

A decision the product owner settled goes back to the product owner, even when the evidence is clear: choosing its replacement is a choice too.

**Escalate.**
1. Sync, then `compose change start --project <slug> --why "<one line>"`.
2. Start the change's wayfinder with the reflect file, the evidence, and the held tasks: `compose wayfinder --project <slug> --change <id> --extra - <<'EOF' | compose crew start --project <slug> --role wayfinder --title "change <id>" --brief -`.
3. When it reports `needs-user`, report `needs-user "change <id> on <slug>: <what was found>. Held <ids>; the rest keeps running. Questions: <page>. Recommended: <...>"`.
4. Evidence that arrives while the change is open goes to that teammate as new cards: `herdr agent prompt <slug>-wayfinder "<evidence>"`.
5. When it reports `done`, re-plan.

**Re-plan.**
1. If no change is open: sync, then `compose change start --why "<one line>"`.
2. The planner teammate edits Linear: `## Plan`; it cancels the tasks that rest on the old line (a canceled task loses its `compose` block), edits the briefs that change, and adds new tasks with the next ids, and `compose testcrews --only <new ids>` for their tests.
3. The 3b gate applies unchanged: a list that matches what the product owner just settled dispatches at once, and anything past it goes back up.
4. Sync, then `compose change done`: it comments the change on the feature.
5. `compose hold --release <ids>`, then `compose run --only <new and redone ids> --redo` for the layers built on the old line.

**The delivery plan.**
The planner rewrites its judgment sections (Outside waits, How this maps to compose, Priority when something has to give) at each re-plan, with `compose linear doc update <id> --section`.
Priority is the ranked list under Priority when something has to give; Linear's priority field stays unused.
The team lead and the engineering manager never edit the delivery plan by hand: the CLI rewrites its data sections and the planner its judgment sections.

**Undo and update.**
- `compose change undo <id>` opens a new change that puts back every issue and task branch the change touched; sync, then `change done`. It refuses when something it touched has changed since: undo the later change first.
- An update is a new change whose reason names the one it updates: `change start --why "update C2: <why>"`.
